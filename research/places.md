## JAPAN — the Shikoku 88 (四国八十八箇所)

The route rings one island, roughly 1,100 to 1,400 km, about 40 days on foot.
Four prefectures, four stages: Tokushima = resolve, Kōchi = training,
Ehime = enlightenment, Kagawa = nirvana. Between 2,500 and 5,000 people walk
the whole thing each year, out of 100,000+ who visit by other means.

Visiting a temple is called 打つ *utsu*, "to strike", because name slips were
once nailed to the gate. Finishing is 結願 *kechigan*. Many never finish, and
that is not a failure.

**同行二人 dōgyō ninin** — "two people, walking together". Written on the hat
and the staff. However alone you are, you walk with Kōbō Daishi. Some walk with
a dead parent instead. This phrase is the feature.

### What a pilgrim carries (all strong simple shapes)

- **菅笠 sugegasa**, the hat. A flat cone. Carries 同行二人 and a verse that was
  originally written on coffin lids: "Because we are deluded, the three worlds
  are a prison. Because we awaken, the ten directions are empty. Fundamentally
  there is no east or west. Where then is there north or south?"
- **金剛杖 kongōzue**, the staff. Square in section, ~130 cm, head carved as a
  five-ring stupa and wrapped in brocade. Treated as Kōbō Daishi himself: washed
  before the walker washes, given the seat of honour indoors, never cut. Never
  tapped when crossing a bridge, because he once slept under one.
- **納経帳 nōkyōchō**, the stamp book. Three red seals plus black brush
  calligraphy per temple. Not a souvenir: a receipt for an offering.
- **納札 osamefuda**, name slips left at each hall. White for 1 to 4 circuits,
  green 5 to 7, red 8 to 24, silver 25 to 49, gold 50 to 99, brocade for 100+.
  Nobody is required to upgrade.
- **お接待 settai**, gifts from strangers to pilgrims: food, drink, a bed. You
  are not supposed to refuse, because refusing denies the giver their merit.
  The right response is to hand over one of your name slips.
- **The pointing-hand stones** carved by 中務茂兵衛 Nakatsukasa Mohei, who walked
  the route about 280 times and left over 200 waymarks, each with a carved hand.

## CHINA — sites tied to the lines already in the app

Note: Chinese practice is not an ordered numbered circuit like Shikoku. It is
朝山, going to a mountain. Do not force the Japanese model onto it.

| Place | Where | What you see | The one shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| 雲居山真如禪寺 Zhenru, Yunju Shan | Jiangxi | A summit **basin**: flat ground ringed by rounded peaks like lotus petals, mist pooled inside, a spring-fed mirror lake at the centre, monks' fields and tea | The ring of peaks with mist inside and flat water below. The inverse of every other mountain temple |
| 臨濟寺 Linji Si | Zhengding, Hebei | Dead-flat North China plain, a small walled town, the Hutuo river south. Four unlike brick pagodas rise above the walls | A slim blue-grey octagonal nine-eave pagoda, 30.5 m, 1180s |
| 白馬寺 Baima Si | Luoyang, Henan | Flat Luo river floodplain, one soft loess ridge to the north. Traditionally the first Buddhist temple in China, 68 CE | A square thirteen-eave brick pagoda with an inward-curving profile, standing apart from the temple. Two stone horses at the gate |
| 大慈恩寺 / 大雁塔 | Xi'an, Shaanxi | Flat plain, now swallowed by the city. The Qinling wall far to the south | The Big Wild Goose Pagoda: heavy square stepped taper, seven storeys, ~64 m, begun 652 for the scriptures Xuanzang carried from India |

Xuyun 虛雲 rebuilt Zhenru at the age of 114 and died there in 1959. He revived
農禪並重, farming and Chan together, and the rule 一日不作，一日不食 —
a day without work is a day without eating. That line is already in the frog's
satchel.
